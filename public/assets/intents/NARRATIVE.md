Jonathan "Jonie" Gesalem — Narrative Report
The Man
Jonathan Gesalem has been in Cebu's food industry for over 30 years. He opened the first Dunkin' Donuts store at Fuente Osmeña on December 15, 1986 — and he's watched the whole thing grow from that single outlet into what it is today. SunStar He goes by "Jonie" to everyone who knows him. His Chinese name, visible on his business cards, is 葉長青 (Yap Tiong Tsing). His full registered name is Jonathan U. Gesalem, with the "U" likely from his mother's side.
His title on the calling card says VP and COO, but by every measure — press mentions, business registrations, public-facing interviews — he is the man running the show. People who've worked under him describe him as a mentor and big brother figure. One former employee, who eventually became a marketing manager under him, spoke about how working for Gesalem fundamentally shaped how she understood business. INQUIRER.net That's not nothing — that's the kind of reputation that sticks.
On the civic side, he served as President of the Rotary Club of Cebu The Freeman — one of the oldest Rotary clubs in Asia. His Rotary membership card (the one in your uploads) marks him as President for RY 2012–2013, District 3860. He is also listed as a Past President and Major Donor Level 3 to the Rotary Foundation — one of only two in the club to have established a New Endowment Fund that year. Rotarycebu That's a significant personal financial commitment to the foundation, not a ceremonial title.

The Gesalem Group — The Food Empire
The anchor of everything is the Dunkin' franchise operation.
Queen City Food Chain Inc. — known locally as Dunkin' Donuts Cebu — is the biggest Dunkin' franchisee in the region. It operates around 70 outlets in Cebu, with additional branches in Bohol, Iligan, Butuan, and Cagayan de Oro. The company runs under the Gesalem-Huang Group of Companies. Philstar.com
From the calling card, the Dunkin' franchise footprint runs across multiple separate corporate entities by geography:

The Queen City Food Chain, Inc. — Cebu
Golden Era Food Chain, Inc. — Cagayan de Oro
Oro Grande Food Chain, Inc. — Iligan
Rio Grande Food Chain, Inc. — Butuan
Montana De Oro Food Chain, Inc. — Bukidnon

Each is its own company, but the same family runs the whole network. This structure is very typical of how Filipino-Chinese business families operate — they don't just expand a single company, they build new ones per region to manage liability and local relationships.

Jonie's Sizzlers + Roast & Mix Eat
Jonie's Sizzlers + Roast was established in 2000 and has been running for 25 years. The brand is known for Filipino-style comfort food with a twist — international, Asian, and Western dishes done with a Filipino touch. As of 2025, it's finally open for franchising. Cebu Daily News
Mix-Eat is a fast-casual offshoot of Jonie's — same quality, premium look and feel, but at a more accessible price point. With a solid presence across Metro Cebu and Bohol, both brands are positioning for Visayas, Mindanao, and eventually Luzon. Cebu Daily News
Both brands are now being helmed in operations by his son Jiaan, but Jonathan remains the face.

Dragon Fireworks
Jonathan Gesalem holds the position of VP for VisMin (Visayas-Mindanao) operations at Dragon Fireworks. Philstar.com His son Jiaan is listed as COO of Dragon Fireworks as well — by profession a licensed civil engineer, he's described Dragon Fireworks as leveling up the fireworks experience in Cebu, to the point where Cebuanos don't need to go abroad for world-class displays. SunStar Dragon Fireworks handles pyrotechnics for events, celebrations, and likely government contracts across the region.

The Yap Fraternity (Cebu), Inc.
This is the one you have to understand in context. It's not a college fraternity. It's a clan association — specifically for families of the Yeh/Yap lineage, rooted in Fujian, China.
The Cebu chapter was founded in 1970, with the national organization going back to 1946. It recently celebrated its Golden 50th Anniversary in May 2025. Jonie serves as president of the Cebu chapter.
Their work includes Chinese New Year dragon and lion dances, outreach programs to fire victims, medical relief during the pandemic (partnered with IBP Cebu), and disaster relief in Compostela. The Rotary Club of Cebu has publicly thanked the Yap Fraternity as one of its project partners Rotarycebu — which speaks to how embedded Jonie and the organization are in Cebu's civic ecosystem.

The Full Picture
What you're dealing with is a man who started at the ground floor — a single donut shop in 1986 — and built, over four decades, a diversified group of companies across food, construction, fireworks, furniture, and design. He's done it without press fanfare. He built it through relationships, consistency, and trust. His children are now running operations across the portfolio, which means the group is generational. He mentors employees who go on to build their own businesses. He gives back through Rotary, through the Yap Fraternity, through direct outreach.
That's the story. That's who the website needs to represent.
